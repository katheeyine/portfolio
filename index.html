about_me

Hi, I'm Katherine.

I'm a data engineer at an online retailer that sells on several marketplaces across three Southeast Asian countries. I look after the data from start to finish. That means the daily jobs that pull it in, the database where it lands, and the numbers people read on the dashboard each morning.

I studied Computer Science at the Polytechnic University of the Philippines as a DOST scholar, and was part of the Data Science team of Google Developer Student Clubs there.

The lesson I keep coming back to came from a stock figure that jumped to more than ten times its usual level overnight. The maths behind it was right. The data feeding it had collapsed. Since then I hold a number back whenever its input is broken, even when the calculation is correct.

Outside work, I play Valorant, watch anime, K-dramas and movies, and travel whenever I can. I love discovering new places and trying new things.

selected_work

Three things I built, and how I knew they were right

Each card starts with the problem. Flip it to see what I did.

warehouse · forms

From a paper count to a live dashboard

the problem

Warehouse stock counts were done on paper. When a count didn't match the system, the difference often went nowhere.

warehouse · forms

what I did

Built a simple form for the people doing the count, a table behind it, and a dashboard tile. Every difference now becomes a task for the person who has to fix it.

how I checked it

Ran it as a one-week trial with the warehouse team, kept a list of every bug they hit, and fixed them during the trial.

finance · three currencies

Profit you can actually see

the problem

The dashboard showed sales, but not what was left after fees, shipping, tax and the cost of the goods.

finance · three currencies

what I did

Built a profit figure for three countries, each with its own tax rules and currency. It shows a live estimate each day, then the confirmed figure once the marketplace pays out.

how I checked it

Agreed the definition with leadership before building anything, then checked every currency. One cost field turned out not to be in the currency its name suggested, and it affected every margin figure in two countries.

monitoring

The feed that went quiet

the problem

Several numbers on the company dashboard had stopped updating for weeks. Nothing raised an alarm, because every job still reported success.

monitoring

what I did

Traced each number back to the point where it broke, instead of trusting the green status lights.

how I checked it

Now every job has to show the data it delivered, every day. A success message on its own doesn't count.

how_i_work

Four habits behind every number

  • Check the live system, not the notes.Documents drift. The data itself is the answer.
  • Count what arrived.A job can report success and deliver nothing, so I look at the rows that actually landed.
  • Read what fills a field before trusting its name.A column called "Secondary Category" once turned out to hold supplier names.
  • Agree the definition before building.Half of a good business number is everyone meaning the same thing by it.

undergraduate thesis · machine learning

XAVIER

A model that classifies fungal skin infections into their types. It reads an image, adds the symptoms you can't see in a picture, and then shows how it reached its answer.

  • cnn
  • efficientnet-b3
  • decision-tree
  • lime
  • grad-cam

how it works

  • A CNN (EfficientNet-B3) reads the image.
  • A decision tree handles the symptoms that don't show up in an image.
  • LIME and Grad-CAM highlight what the model looked at, so a person can check its reasoning instead of taking the answer on trust.

automata theory

Résumé skill matcher

A prototype that screens résumés automatically. It reads the text letter by letter and matches skills with a deterministic finite automaton (DFA), a small machine that moves between states as it reads.

  • parsing

try the idea

Each matching letter moves the machine one state closer to the end. A small demo of the idea, made for this page.
